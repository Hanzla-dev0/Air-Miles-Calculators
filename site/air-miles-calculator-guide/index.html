How Air Miles Are Calculated

The maths behind every air miles calculator, explained in plain English.

Great-circle distance

An air miles calculator reports the shortest distance between two airports across the surface of the Earth. This is called the great-circle distance, because the shortest path lies along a circle whose centre is the centre of the Earth. On a flat map it looks curved; on a globe it is a straight line pulled tight.

The haversine formula

Given the latitude (φ) and longitude (λ) of two airports, the haversine formula is:

a = sin²(Δφ / 2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ / 2)
c = 2 · atan2(√a, √(1 − a))
distance = R · c

Here R is the Earth's mean radius: 3,958.8 miles, 6,371 kilometres or 3,440 nautical miles, depending on the unit you want.

Worked example: JFK to LHR

John F. Kennedy International Airport sits at 40.64° N, 73.78° W and Heathrow Airport at 51.47° N, 0.45° W. Putting those coordinates through the formula gives 3,443 air miles (5,540 km). You can check it on the New York to London distance page.

Why flights cover more than the air miles distance

  • Airways and air-traffic control: aircraft follow published routes.
  • Weather and winds: crews route around storms and towards favourable jet streams.
  • Restricted airspace: some regions must be avoided.
  • Safety rules: long over-water routes must keep suitable diversion airports within range.

Air miles, kilometres and nautical miles

One statute mile is 1.609 km and about 0.869 nautical miles. Pilots and air-traffic controllers work in nautical miles, but travel sites and many loyalty schemes use statute miles, which is why this air miles calculator shows all three.

Estimating flight time

A simple rule of thumb divides the distance by 500 mph and adds half an hour for taxi, take-off and landing. It is a good planning estimate; the timetable of your airline is always the final word.

Frequently asked questions

What formula does an air miles calculator use?

Most use the haversine formula, which gives the great-circle distance between two points from their latitude and longitude on a sphere.

Is the great-circle distance exact?

It is very close. The Earth is slightly flattened, so more complex ellipsoidal formulas can differ by up to roughly 0.5%, which is small for travel planning.

Do airlines use air miles to award frequent-flyer points?

Some do, but many now award points by fare, cabin and spend. Always check the rules of your loyalty programme.
